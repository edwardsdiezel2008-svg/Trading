⚡ Leverage & liquidation

—

This track sizes every position at — notional exposure per dollar of equity - the same price moves as the spot — track, amplified. A much smaller adverse price swing is enough to force-close a leg here than the unleveraged tracks' flat 50%-loss floor requires; "Distance to liquidation" is how far the latest price sits from that force-close level right now. Backtest gap risk: the engine only checks for a liquidation-triggering loss at each bar's close, not continuously - a single bar large enough can jump straight past the threshold, showing as an outsized loss rather than a clean stop at the liquidation price. That's a known simplification, not a bug, and it's the reason a leveraged strategy's equity can occasionally show as more deeply negative than the liquidation math alone would suggest. Funding is the periodic payment perpetual contracts exchange between longs and shorts to stay anchored to spot price - Crypto.com Exchange recalculates the rate every 4 hours and settles it hourly against open positions; this track applies the live rate once per hourly check-in, matching that cadence, and tracks it as a real running total. It is not included in the Total Return / equity figures above (which come from the historical backtest), since Crypto.com's public API only exposes the current funding rate, not a historical series that could be backtested honestly.

📈 Nasdaq Futures, honestly

Both Nasdaq Futures tracks trade the real NQ=F price (CME Nasdaq-100 E-mini futures, continuous front contract), fetched from Yahoo Finance's public chart endpoint - unauthenticated and keyless, the same way Crypto.com's REST API supplies crypto candles here without needing an account. That real futures price is paired with the real Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 (MNQ) contract's point-value economics ($2/index-point multiplier, $0.75/side commission) - MNQ and NQ track the identical index level, just at 1/10th the contract size, so this is real futures P&L math, not an ETF-proxy approximation.

Real MNQ futures currently require roughly $1,700-1,900 in CME initial margin against a notional value in the tens of thousands of dollars per contract - implied leverage in the ~25-30x range, set by the exchange, not a discretionary choice like the crypto perpetual tracks' 3x. Both tracks here run unleveraged instead (position size is simply however many whole MNQ contracts $100,000 of paper capital can cover): the backtest engine only checks for a liquidation-triggering loss at each bar's close, and modeling ~25-30x real margin risk against bar-close-only checks would produce either constant false liquidations or a threshold so loose it stops meaning anything. The real margin/leverage figures above are sourced context, not something simulated.

The 5-Minute track's data has one retention limit worth knowing: Yahoo's intraday endpoint only serves the trailing ~60 days per request. Every hourly run merges freshly-fetched bars into the existing history, so the 5-minute track's own record keeps accumulating past that 60-day window going forward - same pattern already used for the memecoin scanner's precise-history coins.

Robustness (walk-forward)

—

The full-backtest numbers above are one long in-sample fit - a strategy can look great by luck alone over one history. Walk-forward re-optimizes each strategy's parameters on rolling training windows and scores it only on data it never saw during that fit. A strategy that's robust here made money on genuinely unseen data, not just in hindsight. Param stability is a separate check: the % of nearby parameter values (e.g. a lookback of 18 or 22 instead of 20) that were also profitable - a real edge tends to hold up across a plateau of similar settings, not just the one exact default.

🚩 Rug Pull Watch

—

Flags coins with a severe 24h decline, or a severe drawdown from a recent high - the price-action aftermath a rug pull leaves behind (liquidity drained, price collapses), not the on-chain cause. The 11 coins with full hourly history (see the precise scanner above) are also checked against their own trailing ~12-day high, not just the last 24h, so a multi-day slide gets caught even on a day that looks unremarkable by itself. This exchange is a curated, mainstream listing where a literal on-chain rug pull (LP drain, honeypot contract) is rare - most flags here will be ordinary high-volatility drawdowns, a broad market selloff, or a thin-liquidity air pocket, not an actual rug. There's no holder-concentration or liquidity-lock data behind this - it's a crash proxy from price/volume alone, not a verdict. "Flagged for" tracks how many consecutive hourly checks a coin has stayed flagged - a persistent flag is a stronger signal than a one-off dip.
