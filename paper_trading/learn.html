What this actually is

A fully autonomous paper-trading research system. A GitHub Actions pipeline checks in on real BTC/ETH/SOL, Nasdaq futures, and memecoin market data roughly once an hour, runs 12 backtested strategies against it per asset, and rebuilds this site - all independent of anyone having a chat session open. No real money is ever at risk: every position, fill, and P&L figure here is simulated against real prices, not placed on a real exchange.

The point isn't to promise a strategy that works. It's to be honest about which of 12 reasonable, well-known trading ideas actually hold up against real data, and to show the evidence - including the ones that don't hold up - rather than curate a highlight reel.

The 12 strategies
MA_CrossoverTrend

Long while the fast moving average (10) sits above the slow one (50), short while it sits below. The oldest trend-following idea there is.

Needs: a market that trends for a while once it starts moving.

Donchian_BreakoutBreakout

Long on a close above the 20-bar high, short on a close below the 20-bar low - the "turtle trading" channel breakout.

Needs: breakouts that follow through instead of immediately reversing.

MACD_MomentumTrend

Trades the sign of the MACD histogram - the gap between a 12/26-period EMA difference and its own 9-period signal line.

Needs: sustained momentum, not chop.

RSI_ReversionMean-Reversion

Buys when 14-period RSI drops under 30 (oversold), shorts when it tops 70 (overbought), on the bet that extremes snap back.

Needs: a range-bound market - actively hurt by strong trends.

Bollinger_ReversionMean-Reversion

Fades a close outside the 20-period, 2-standard-deviation band back toward its own moving-average midline.

Needs: volatility that mean-reverts rather than expands.

ZScore_ReversionMean-Reversion

Same idea as Bollinger Reversion, expressed as a raw statistical z-score of price vs. its 20-period mean instead of a band.

Needs: a range-bound market.

ATR_Vol_BreakoutBreakout

Enters in the direction of a move larger than 1.5x the 14-period Average True Range - a volatility-scaled breakout instead of a fixed price level.

Needs: volatility expansions that mark the start of a real move.

SupertrendTrend

A trailing ATR band (10-period, 3.0x multiplier) that only ever ratchets in the trend's favor - price closing through it flips the position.

Needs: a trend, with a built-in trailing stop baked into the signal itself.

VWAP_ReversionMean-Reversion

Fades price more than 2% away from a rolling volume-weighted average price - the only one of the indicator-based strategies that actually uses trading volume, not just close price.

Needs: a range-bound market where volume-weighted fair value holds.

Engulfing_ReversalPattern

A two-candle reversal: a bullish engulfing candle (green body fully covering the prior red candle's body) after a down move signals long; bearish engulfing signals short.

Needs: real conviction behind the reversal candle - weak, doji-like bodies are filtered out.

Inside_Bar_BreakoutPattern

Trades a consolidation-then-breakout setup: an "inside bar" contained entirely within the prior "mother" bar's range, followed by a close beyond that mother bar's high or low.

Needs: a genuine pause before the move, not constant chop.

Opening_Range_BreakoutPattern

The standard 5-minute ORB day-trading setup: the first few bars of each session set an "opening range," and a breakout of that range confirmed by VWAP triggers an entry held until session end.

Needs: an intraday timeframe with real session structure - correctly never fires on daily bars.

How we test before trusting a strategy
1

Full-backtest return. Every strategy's total return over the whole available price history, starting from $100,000. This alone is a weak signal - it's one long in-sample fit, and a strategy can look great by luck over one specific history.

2

Walk-forward validation. Parameters get re-optimized on a rolling training window and scored only on the data right after it, which the fit never saw. A strategy is only called "robust" here if it made money out-of-sample, not just in hindsight.

3

Parameter stability. A real edge tends to hold up across a plateau of nearby settings (a lookback of 18 or 22, not just exactly 20). We sweep nearby parameter values and report the fraction that were also profitable - a strategy that only works at one exact number is more likely overfit.

4

Cross-asset robustness. A strategy that's walk-forward-robust on BTC alone could just be that one price history getting lucky. We check whether it's independently robust on ETH and SOL too - three unrelated histories agreeing is much harder to explain by chance.

Reading the numbers
Total Return

Ending equity vs. the $100,000 starting capital, over the full backtest history.

Max Drawdown

The single largest peak-to-trough decline in a strategy's equity curve. Two strategies can end with the same total return and have very different rides getting there - this is the "how bad could it get" number that total return alone hides.

Win Rate

% of closed trades that were profitable. A low win rate isn't automatically bad - trend-followers often win under half their trades but let the winners run far larger than the losers.

Profit Factor

Gross profit divided by gross loss. Above 1.0 means profitable overall even with a low win rate; the natural companion stat to Win Rate.

Out-of-Sample Sharpe

Return per unit of volatility, measured only on the walk-forward test windows a strategy's parameters were never fit to.

Duration

How long a closed trade was held, entry to exit - tells apart a scalp (minutes to hours) from a multi-day swing at a glance.

Buy & Hold benchmark

What simply buying at the start and holding would have returned - the bar every active strategy has to actually beat to justify trading at all.

Regime (Trending / Ranging)

Classified from ADX on recent price action. Trend-following strategies need a trending regime to have anything to catch; mean-reversion strategies need a ranging one.

Leverage

Position notional as a multiple of equity - every perpetual futures track runs at 3x, so the same price move produces roughly 3x the gain or loss of that track's spot equivalent.

Liquidation Price

The price at which a leveraged position gets force-closed because it's lost too much of the margin backing it - the leveraged-specific risk the spot tracks don't have at all. "Distance to liquidation" on the BTC Perpetual tab is how far the current price sits from that level.

Funding Accrued

A real running total of the periodic payment perpetual contracts exchange between longs and shorts, using the live rate fetched each hourly check-in. Tracked separately from Total Return/Equity, not blended into them - see "BTC Perpetual, honestly" below.

Locked-in Strategy

The single strategy on a track, if any, currently profitable on the full backtest, walk-forward-robust, and parameter-stable all at once - computed live from that track's robustness data, not hand-picked. See "Locked-in strategy, honestly" below.

Meta-Strategy Selector

A regime-conditional recommendation: which strategy has historically performed best specifically in the market's current trend/volatility regime, walk-forward validated fold by fold. Different question than Locked-in Strategy (overall track record vs. regime-specific edge). See "Meta-strategy selector, honestly" below.

NQ=F / MNQ

NQ=F is Yahoo Finance's ticker for the real CME Nasdaq-100 E-mini futures continuous contract - the actual price both Nasdaq Futures tabs trade. MNQ is the Micro E-mini version of the same contract: identical index price, 1/10th the point-value multiplier, which is the contract economics used for P&L math here.

Perpetual futures, honestly

Four tabs (BTC Perpetual, BTC Perp 15-Min, ETH Perpetual, SOL Perpetual) run the full strategy library at 3x leverage against the same price history as their spot equivalent - same price moves, amplified gains and losses. 3x sits inside the 1x-5x leverage range widely recommended for beginners on crypto perpetuals. A few things worth being upfront about:

Leverage doesn't just scale returns by 3x. It changes the compounding dynamics: a position that would have simply drawn down and recovered unleveraged can instead get liquidated at 3x, permanently losing the capital that would have ridden out the recovery. The ETH and SOL Perpetual tabs have consistently shown far fewer profitable strategies than their unleveraged spot equivalents (see the live tabs for current counts) - the more volatile an asset, the harder leverage punishes exactly this dynamic. That's a real finding, not a bug in the simulation - compare any strategy's Total Return here against its spot-track equivalent and it's frequently lower, not higher.

Funding cost isn't backtested, only tracked going forward. Crypto.com's public API only exposes the current funding rate per asset, not a historical series, so there's no honest way to reconstruct what funding would have cost over the multi-year backtest period. The "Funding Accrued" figure is real, not simulated - it accrues from each asset's own live rate at each hourly check-in (BTC, ETH, and SOL funding rates are fetched and tracked independently, since each reflects that asset's own long/short positioning) - but it only started counting from when this feature launched, and it's shown separately from Total Return/Equity rather than silently folded into them. Crypto.com Exchange recalculates funding every 4 hours and settles it hourly against open positions, so applying the live rate once per hourly check-in matches its real cadence rather than approximating it.

Nasdaq Futures, honestly

The two Nasdaq Futures tabs (Daily and 5-Min) run the full strategy library against the real NQ=F price - CME's Nasdaq-100 E-mini futures, continuous front contract - fetched from Yahoo Finance's public, keyless chart endpoint the same way Crypto.com Exchange's public API supplies crypto candles here without needing an account. That real price is paired with the real Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 (MNQ) contract's point-value economics ($2/index-point multiplier, $0.75/side commission) - MNQ tracks the identical index level as full-size NQ, just at 1/10th the contract size, so this is genuine futures P&L math rather than an ETF-proxy approximation. A few things worth being upfront about:

Why unleveraged. Real MNQ futures currently require roughly $1,700-1,900 in CME initial margin against a notional value in the tens of thousands of dollars per contract - implied leverage in the ~25-30x range, set by the exchange, not a discretionary choice like the crypto perpetual tabs' 3x. Both tabs deliberately don't attempt to simulate that: the backtest engine only checks for a liquidation-triggering loss at each bar's close, and modeling ~25-30x real margin risk against bar-close-only checks - even on 5-minute bars - would produce either constant false liquidations or a threshold so loose it stops meaning anything. Both tabs run unleveraged instead, so real futures' leverage and liquidation risk are sourced context here, not something simulated.

5-minute data retention. Yahoo's intraday endpoint only serves the trailing ~60 days per request - a Yahoo policy, not a choice made here. Every hourly run merges freshly-fetched bars into the 5-Min tab's own history, so it keeps accumulating past that 60-day window over time, same as every other track here.

Pattern-recognition strategies, honestly

Three strategies in the library read the raw shape of the candles rather than an indicator series, added specifically because intraday timeframes - the 5-minute Nasdaq Futures tab most of all - show far more of these setups per session than a daily chart does. All three are standard, publicly documented price-action setups:

Engulfing Reversal - a two-candle reversal: a bullish engulfing candle (a green candle whose body fully engulfs the prior red candle's body) after a down move signals long; a bearish engulfing candle signals short.

Inside Bar Breakout - a consolidation-then-breakout setup: an "inside bar" (a bar whose entire range sits inside the prior "mother" bar's range) followed by a close beyond the mother bar's high or low.

Opening Range Breakout - the standard 5-minute ORB day-trading setup: the high/low of the first few bars of each session becomes the "opening range"; a breakout of that range, confirmed by sitting on the same side of the session's cumulative VWAP, triggers an entry held until the opposite breakout or session end. On daily bars this correctly never fires - ORB is an inherently intraday concept.

Locked-in strategy, honestly

Every tab now shows a "🔒 Locked-in strategy" call-out above the full comparison table, computed live in the browser from that track's walk-forward and sensitivity results: the one strategy, if any, that's simultaneously profitable on the full backtest, walk-forward-robust on out-of-sample data, and stable across nearby parameter values. If more than one clears all three bars, the highest out-of-sample Sharpe wins; if none do, the panel says so rather than picking one anyway. This is computed fresh from whatever robustness data exists for that track - it needs an actual walk-forward + sensitivity snapshot on file first, so a brand new track (like the 5-minute Nasdaq Futures tab right after launch) will read "not enough history yet" until real data accumulates and a snapshot is run.

Meta-strategy selector, honestly

The "🧠 Meta-strategy selector" panel answers a different question than Locked-in Strategy above: not "which single strategy has the best overall track record," but "which strategy should be trusted given the current market regime." It's built directly on the existing regime classifier (ADX-based trend/volatility detection, the same one behind the Regime pill on the landing page) - no new detection logic, just a decision layer that learns, per regime, which strategy historically performed best while that regime was in effect.

How it avoids just being another way to curve-fit. The regime -> strategy mapping is learned only on a TRAIN window and applied, unmodified, to the following unseen TEST window - the same walk-forward discipline used everywhere else on this site. A regime only gets a strategy assignment if it had enough bars of TRAIN history and the best strategy found there was actually net profitable in that regime; thinner or unprofitable regimes are left unassigned, and the selector goes flat rather than guessing.

The honest limitation, stated up front. This runs on a genuinely small dataset by ML standards - a few thousand bars, a handful of regime buckets, a handful of assets - which makes a learned mapping like this easier to overfit than the simple rule-based strategies it's choosing between, not harder. The safeguards above reduce that risk; they don't eliminate it. Real, observed out-of-sample results across the tracks this has been run against range from +80.6% (BTC Daily) to -84.9% (ETH Daily) - a genuinely honest spread, not a cherry-picked one, and proof that "regime-aware" doesn't automatically mean "better." Treat every number this panel shows with the same skepticism as any other walk-forward result.

Rug Pull Watch, honestly

The memecoin scanner's Rug Pull Watch panel flags coins with a severe 24-hour decline, a severe drawdown from their 24h high, or (for the 11 coins with full hourly history) a slow multi-day slide from their own trailing high. That's a price-action crash proxy from CEX ticker data - the aftermath a rug pull leaves behind - not on-chain rug detection. There's no holder-concentration or liquidity-lock signal behind it, and this exchange is a curated, mainstream listing where an actual on-chain rug pull (LP drain, honeypot contract) is rare. Most flags are ordinary high-volatility drawdowns or thin-liquidity air pockets, not literal rugs.

The pipeline

A GitHub Actions workflow runs on a schedule (roughly hourly), independent of any browser tab or chat session being open. Each run: fetches fresh BTC/ETH/SOL and memecoin candles plus order book, funding rate, Fear & Greed, and news data from public sources (mainly Crypto.com Exchange's REST API); updates every paper-trading track's simulated positions; refreshes the memecoin scanners; rebuilds both HTML pages; commits the result; and redeploys to GitHub Pages. This page you're reading is served from that same deploy.

Costs are modeled, not ignored: every simulated crypto trade pays Crypto.com's published taker fee (0.075%) plus an assumed 0.05% slippage, both round-trip - a strategy has to clear that bar on every single trade, not just be directionally right.

Caveats worth repeating
  • Simulated, not real money. No trades are ever actually placed anywhere. Nothing here is investment advice.
  • Hourly, not real-time. The system checks in on the market roughly once an hour; it doesn't react intraday beyond that cadence.
  • New tracks are noisy. A strategy with only a few dozen bars of history can look great or terrible by chance alone - the dashboard flags this explicitly wherever a track is still thin on data.
  • The backtest engine doesn't model margin calls. Unconstrained short position sizing can push a simulated account's equity to or below zero over a long enough history - the dashboard labels this "wiped out" rather than showing a confusing negative dollar figure.
